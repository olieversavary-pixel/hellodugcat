# Personal Reflections on Learning Drawing and Design, Computer Programming, Violin, and Other Fields
  
  - The Internet has broken down cognitive barriers, enabling things to happen today that were unthinkable in the Middle Ages. What? You think this is unfair to people across different eras? Haha, don't overthink it — after all, on some level, every era has its own set of crises.
  
  # Different Crises, Different Shackles
  
  - From our own macro perspective, our personal crises are limited to physical perception, social interests, and self-isolation. Yet the shift of the times has dialed up the difficulty — adding **information-induced hallucinations** and the rigorous training of rational tools.
  
  - In this era, the volume of information grows exponentially, evolving in a multi-threaded fashion — a true flourishing of a hundred flowers. But while information has multiplied, so has trash. I firmly believe that high-quality information is like the noble ideals of those friends I have never met... if not handled properly, it will inevitably be contaminated by utilitarianism and **obedience**.
  
  - Therefore, a few questions need answers:
  
    1. How to strengthen one's own stability  
    2. How to filter and synthesize high-quality resources tailored to oneself  
  
  - Think about it! **This will inevitably belong to you.** Hahaha!
  
  # The Possibility of Renaissance-Style Learning
  
  - If so, driven purely by our innate curiosity, who knows how many Renaissance-style, well-rounded polymaths would emerge in this world? Though I say this only half-jokingly.
  
  - First, let me make a broad classification of human knowledge — natural sciences, social sciences, humanities, formal sciences, and engineering sciences. The subfields within each major domain connect with one another and give birth to new fields, which serves as our key entry point for mastering rapid learning.
  
  - In my previous article, I discussed the concept of isomorphism. Now, if I break down the problem I'm facing into a single discipline and examine it in isolation, it will likely exhibit a clear linear trajectory: **Fundamentals (building initial neural circuits) → Intermediate (forming low-energy models and myelination) → Advanced (broad neural pathways)**. However, once we simplify and process this domain's model (through mapping, projection, etc.) and transfer it to other domains, you will discover that other knowledge is nothing extraordinary (the power of meta-structures). Thus, what matters most is not mere intellectual understanding, but how to instruct the body — through well-structured deliberate practice, forming muscle memory.
  
  - This is also my core motivation for bringing up violin, drawing, and similar pursuits:
  
    - When I took on the challenge of learning drawing from scratch, I absorbed a massive amount of theoretical knowledge (form, color, spatial positioning). This gave me an overwhelming sense of confidence and security, causing me to overlook training and external feedback (which is incidentally the sole drawback of self-study). As a result, even **real professionals** couldn't find flaws in my **broad theoretical knowledge**... yet my actual output fell far short... A pity, isn't it?
  
    - The violin is even more demanding — it engages far more senses: your vision, fingers, palms, shoulders, arms, and indeed your entire body. Your ears must be tuned to the absolute limit, alongside separate left and right hand control (I realized the left and right hands aren't truly acting as one!). I suppose this is what true embodied experience feels like...
  
  # The Unity of Disciplines and Professions
  
  - Real-world jobs are merely labels used for positioning; just like academic disciplines, they don't need to be rigidly compartmentalized... What matters is discovering your own 'habits' within your work. Look: doctors and teachers are equivalent; hackers and psychologists are equivalent; mathematicians and artists are equivalent; network engineering and law are equivalent...
  
  # Information, Cognition, and Human Connection
  
  - The dissemination of information breaks through the limits of human cognition. For example, written text detaches experience from specific events, indirectly shaping human choices, the connections between shifts, and the shifting of connections.
